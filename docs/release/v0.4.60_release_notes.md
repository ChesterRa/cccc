# CCCC v0.4.60 Release Notes

CCCC v0.4.60 adds an experimental unattended mode, off by default, in which
Chet handles the messages members send you while you are away. Each browser
can now choose which messages notify you, and message delivery is easier to
trust. When CCCC has the evidence, the recipient list says whether a message
was typed into a member's terminal or accepted by its runtime, and it can show
why a message is still waiting. Deliveries missed during a short disconnection
are recovered. When a restart that keeps agents running leaves two session
owners and one stops responding, running members whose healthy owner CCCC can
verify keep receiving messages and can be stopped on their own, as the v0.4.59
notes promised.

## Upgrading from v0.4.59

Use your usual update method. On Linux and native Apple Silicon Macs, a
compatible update can keep agents running, as in v0.4.59; on Windows, updates
restart agents. Your Groups, settings, messages, tasks and unread Mail are kept.
- New messages to a member kept running from v0.4.59 can still show **Sent to
  runtime**. Restarting the member enables the more specific receipt when it
  is available.
- If you return to v0.4.59, cards closed only by Chet's answers show as waiting
  again; Chet's answers stay in the Group. To pause unattended mode, turn its
  switch off instead.

## Let Chet act for you while you are away (Experimental)
- Chet's title bar has an **Unattended** switch for the whole instance, off by
  default. While it is on, each new message a member of a Group on this
  instance sends you with **Send** or **Send + Reply** still reaches you, with
  your usual notifications, and Chet also gets a copy. Chet can reply to the
  member, answer question cards for you, or point out a better approach or a
  risk; it is instructed to leave to you what only you can do, such as signing
  in or paying. Messages from other instances and IM are not copied. Chet's
  entry below the Group list says **Unattended on**.
- A card Chet answered is marked as answered by Chet for you, with the option
  it chose when the recorded answer is loaded, and counts as answered
  everywhere your pending replies are shown. Reply to Chet's answer to correct
  it: by default your reply goes to the member who asked.
- Chet's conversation shows each copy, marked as a message to you that was
  copied, with a link to Chet's reply when one was recorded in the Group. What
  Chet writes in its own conversation stays there; only the messages it sends
  to the Group appear in the Group.
- While the switch is on, Chet's entry also asks for attention when CCCC finds
  that Chet's Runtime is not ready, for example not installed or waiting for
  setup.
- Only messages sent after the switch is turned on are copied. When it is
  turned off, an already registered copy that has not yet passed CCCC's last
  check before handing it to Chet is recorded as not started, and card answers
  Chet has not written yet are refused; what Chet already wrote stays. A copy
  that already passed that check can still lead to an ordinary reply.
- Copies use Chet's own session, Runtime and quota, and the CCCC Web service
  must be running. They share Chet's session with your private conversation
  with Chet; keeping that conversation out of the Group relies on Chet's
  instructions. Chet handles copies one at a time, so when members send many
  messages at once its replies can be delayed. If Chet runs on OpenCode and
  your own conversation with it still holds a copy back ten minutes after the
  message was sent, that copy is not handed to Chet; the original message
  stays in the Group, and any unanswered card is still yours to answer.
- Chet can misjudge, and members may act on its answer before you correct it.

## Choose which messages notify you
- Settings > Notifications has a new choice for each browser: **All messages
  to me** (as before) or **Only when a reply is requested**. With the second,
  ordinary updates and replies no longer notify you; requests for your reply
  or decision, including choice cards, still do.
- The choice is saved separately for each browser, so a phone can notify you
  only for requests while a computer notifies you for everything. Switching
  back to all messages does not notify you about messages already skipped.
  Browsers that already had notifications on keep **All messages to me**.

## See where a message went
- In the recipient list, a delivered message shows **Written to terminal
  (execution unconfirmed)** or **Accepted by the runtime** when CCCC has that
  evidence. Receipts without it, including older messages, keep **Sent to
  runtime**. Neither label claims the member acted on the message.
- A message that has not been delivered yet can show why it waits: the Group
  is paused or stopped, the member is disabled or switching runtime, or the
  message has not been submitted yet. A delivery that can still be retried is
  no longer shown as failed; **Delivery failed** is kept for final failures.
  Mail the member has already read is never shown as waiting.
- For a plain message, **Show recipient status** in the message's action menu
  opens this list, so you can see where it went or why it waits. The list
  refreshes each time it opens and offers **Send now** for deliveries that are
  eligible.
- `cccc ledger search --with-obligation-status` and an agent reading one
  message by its ID with `with_obligation_status` see the same delivery facts.
  An agent sees its own status on messages it received and its recipients'
  status on messages it sent.

## Recover missed deliveries
- A message whose delivery had not started, or is known not to have been
  submitted, during a short disconnection is delivered when the member is
  back. A full delivery queue now shows the message as waiting and tries again
  later instead of marking it failed. Long backlogs are delivered in full, and
  a Group that fails to reload catches up afterwards.
- When a write to a member's terminal fails partway, the delivery stays
  unconfirmed and CCCC does not type the message a second time.

## Members kept running across a restart
- Stopping one member no longer marks the whole Group stopped when CCCC
  briefly cannot reach another member's process.
- For a member kept running by its session process, Start, Stop, Restart, New
  session, Remove and enabling or disabling it first check that CCCC can reach
  that process. If the check fails, the request is refused and the member,
  its message delivery and its saved session stay as they were.
- PTY input meant for a member's terminal that was just replaced is not typed
  into the new terminal.

## Other fixes
- Uploaded SVG attachments up to 4 MiB preview in chat, the viewer and the
  resource panel instead of showing "cannot preview"; larger ones can still be
  downloaded.
- On a phone, closing the last tab in the resource panel closes the panel.
- OpenCode members keep an `OPENCODE_CONFIG_CONTENT` configuration inherited
  from the environment CCCC was started in; a member's own setting still wins.
- DeepSeek setup reports the managed-session mode.
- Switches, including those in Settings, are easier to read: the knob moves
  right and fills in when a switch is on.
- In developer mode, `debug_snapshot` also reports ledger-index cache counters
  and, on Linux, the daemon's process memory.

## When one of two session owners stops responding

A restart that keeps agents running can leave two session owners: the earlier
process holds the members that kept running, and the newer one holds members
started later. In v0.4.59, if either owner stopped responding, members held by
the other owner stopped receiving new messages and could not be stopped
individually. The v0.4.59 notes promised a fix in this release.

Now, while one owner is unresponsive, running members whose healthy owner
CCCC can verify keep receiving new messages, including notices and messages that ask for a reply,
and each of those members can be stopped on its own. A message to a member of
the unresponsive owner that has not been handed off can remain pending and be
sent after that owner responds. Stopping the whole Group still waits for every
owner's answer.

In v0.4.59, an owner that stayed unresponsive long enough could also stall
background upkeep, such as scheduled rules and catch-up deliveries, for every
Group until it responded again. Background upkeep now continues. A read of the
unresponsive owner can still time out, so each pass can take a few seconds
longer, and if its connection queue is full, the connection fails at once.

For members kept running from v0.4.59, this covers terminal members. Managed
and DeepSeek members kept running from v0.4.59 keep the earlier behavior until
they are restarted.

### Known limitation

If an owner is already unresponsive when CCCC starts, startup can still fail
and name that owner. The daemon log gives its PID. If the owner responds
again, retry `cccc daemon start`. As a last resort, verify the process against
the reported owner ID and PID before ending only that process. Its work can
outlive it. If startup reports unconfirmed work, follow
[When CCCC cannot confirm an agent](../guide/operations.md#when-cccc-cannot-confirm-an-agent)
before retrying.


## Validation

- The changes were peer-reviewed, with a separate review for changes spanning
  several entry points.
- Linux, on this release's source: rustfmt, Clippy and the Rust workspace
  tests in CI's three groups, each of the 4,494 tests that are not ignored
  among the 4,554 listed run once; the Web check and all 5,193 Web tests in 690
  files; and the repository's 128 Python tests.
- Upgrades on a Linux x86-64 trial build: from the published v0.4.59 archive,
  then a restart that kept agents; both times the original terminal member and
  its MCP process were reconnected and the task in progress kept running.
- Two session owners on a Linux x86-64 trial build. Each scenario kept one
  owner hung, holding its lock, from before the first request until the
  healthy member had stopped, while background upkeep kept running. With
  terminal members, either owner was the hung one; when the newer owner hung,
  the healthy member had been kept running from the published v0.4.59 archive.
  Kiro and DeepSeek members, using local ACP stand-ins, were tested with the
  earlier owner hung. One scenario also filled the hung owner's connection
  queue. In every scenario:
  - sending a message and a message asking for a reply returned in 3 to 12 ms,
    and each reached the healthy member once, with an acceptance receipt;
  - with terminal members, an earlier pending notice and the new message
    reached the terminal in order;
  - stopping that member individually worked in 28 to 72 ms;
  - neither request nor the delivery worker contacted the hung owner;
  - after the hung owner responded, its own member received its waiting
    message once.
- Unattended mode: core, daemon and Web tests for which messages are copied
  and when, Chet's answers, and the copies in Chet's conversation, including
  after the switch is turned off or the sender leaves. On a Linux trial build,
  an isolated test supplied Chet's identity and exercised both answers and
  ordinary replies through MCP and the daemon, and an upgrade from the
  published v0.4.59 archive ran in an isolated home. In a separate live trial,
  Chet left progress reports without a reply and answered a question card in
  about a minute.
- In a headless browser with an isolated home: the unattended switch and
  Chet's entry at desktop and phone widths, the recipient list labels and
  waiting reasons, SVG previews, closing the last resource tab on a phone,
  opening a waiting plain message's recipient list from its action menu and
  sending it now, and the notification choice in two separate browser
  profiles, kept after a reload.

Windows and macOS were not tested locally; their CI results are checked before
the release is published. The published packages are built by the release
workflow from the tagged commit.
